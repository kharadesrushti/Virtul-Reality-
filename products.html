          1. Sony PlayStation VR

  


BEST FOR PLAYSTATION 4 OWNERS
The Sony PlayStation VR headset brings powerful, compelling virtual reality, with motion control support, to the PlayStation 4. US Street Price$399.99

    PROS:
    • Immersive VR experience
    • Works with non-VR apps and games
    • Motion control support
    • Low cost of entry compared with PC-based headsets
    CONS:
    • Requires PlayStation Camera, which is not included
    • Slightly less powerful than its main competitors
    • Some motion-tracking hiccups when playing in brightly lit rooms
    • Low cost of entry compared with PC-based headsets







2.Valve Index VR Kit
  



BEST FOR IMMERSIVE PC VR GAMING
The Valve Index is the most impressive consumer VR headset we've seen yet, entirely due to its revolutionary, finger-tracking controllers. US Street Price$999.00

    PROS:
    • Immersive, finger-tracking controllers
    • High, 120Hz refresh rate delivers smooth motion
    • Lots of VR software available on PC via SteamVR
    CONS:
    • Expensive.
    • Slightly less powerful than its main competitors
    • Occasionally frustrating tethered design




3. HTC Vive Pro 2

and don't mind mixing and matching components.US Street Price$799.00

    PROS:

    • Smooth motion tracking
    • Works with Valve Index controllers
    CONS:
    • Expensive
    • Doesn't include necessary base stations or controllers




4. Oculus Quest2
  


OCULUS QUEST 2
The Quest 2 improves over its predecessor in a lot of little ways, with a few small drawbacks like the enlarged controllers and cheaper strap. But its wireless
nature, ease of use, and accessible price still make it a killer VR headset. Price$299.00

    PROS:
    • TExcellent standalone VR
    • Comfortable weight
    • Games and software runs faster and more smoothly
    • Optional PC tethering with accessory cable
    CONS:
    • Standalone games still limited by mobile hardware
    • Only 2-3 hours of battery life
    • Can still cause motion sickness
    • 90Hz isn't available at launch









5. Oculus Rift
  

OCULUS RIFT
$600 is a lot to pay for a piece of gaming hardware. But Rift is different from a console or a new graphics card. Rift allows you to experience games in ways
that we've only dreamed of up until now, and while it requires you to make a few compromises in the name of immersion, it's worth it. Yes, you will look silly
to anyone who watches you use it, and you will potentially feel uneasy after an intense VR session, but these issues can't take away from the fact that Rift
delivers on its promise to enable more immersive and personal gaming experiences than we've ever seen before..

    PROS:
    • Strong support for both sitting and standing experiences
    • Better (sturdier) design than predecessors
    • Built-in 3-D audio makes the VR experiences that much more riveting
    • It's capable of fully immersing you in virtual worlds
    CONS:
    • Requires a powerful gaming PC
    • Slight sound leakage from headphones
    • Xbox controller is a stopgap solution at best
    • Xbox controller is a stopgap solution at best








6. Oculus Go
  











OCULUS GO
The Oculus Go is an affordable, comfortable standalone virtual reality headset that lets you try out VR without making a big investment in hardware.
US Street Price$199.00

    PROS:
    • Relatively affordable
    • No phone, PC, or game system required
    • Cable-free
    • Crisp, fluid display
    CONS:
    • Doesn't track position
    • Just one motion controller
    • Underpowered compared with tethered and flagship smartphone-powered headsets
    • Limited software library
